Arabic roots explained: how to find a word by its root

By The Muhit team · 7 October 2026

What is an Arabic root?

Most Arabic words are built on a root of three consonants (a few have four). The root carries a core meaning. ك ت ب is about writing, د ر س about studying, ع ل م about knowing.

How do patterns build words from a root?

Patterns add vowels, and sometimes extra letters, around the root letters. The same pattern gives a similar kind of meaning across many roots:

  • كَتَبَ he wrote
  • كَاتِب writer (the doer, pattern فَاعِل)
  • مَكْتُوب written, a letter (pattern مَفْعُول)
  • مَكْتَب office, desk (a place, pattern مَفْعَل)
  • مَكْتَبَة library, bookshop
  • كِتَاب book

How do I find the root of an Arabic word?

Remove what the pattern added and keep the consonants that remain:

  • Take off the article ال and endings such as ة, ات, ون and ين.
  • Take off added prefixes such as م, ت and است: اِسْتَخْدَمَ comes from خ د م.
  • Long vowels ا, و and ي are often part of the pattern, not the root: كَاتِب is ك ت ب.
  • When a letter looks missing, the root is usually "weak", with و or ي: قَالَ is ق و ل, مِيزَان is و ز ن.

Why are some Arabic dictionaries sorted by root?

Printed dictionaries such as Hans Wehr group every word under its root, so the whole family sits together. That is excellent for seeing connections, but you have to know the root before you can look a word up. See Muhit vs Hans Wehr.

Looking up roots in Muhit

In Muhit you can type the word as you see it, with or without vowels. Its page shows the root letters; tap them to see every word built on the same root. You can also search the root letters directly.
