Arabic verb conjugation: tenses, moods and the 13 persons

By The Muhit team · 7 October 2026

How many persons does an Arabic verb have?

Thirteen. Arabic distinguishes singular, dual and plural, and masculine and feminine in the second and third persons: أَنَا، نَحْنُ، أَنْتَ، أَنْتِ، أَنْتُمَا، أَنْتُمْ، أَنْتُنَّ، هُوَ، هِيَ، هُمَا، هُمَا، هُمْ، هُنَّ. The third-person dual هُمَا has separate masculine and feminine verb forms, which is why it appears twice.

How is the Arabic past tense formed?

The past tense (الماضي) adds endings to the stem. For كَتَبَ, to write:

أَنَاكَتَبْتُنَحْنُكَتَبْنَا
أَنْتَكَتَبْتَأَنْتِكَتَبْتِ
هُوَكَتَبَهِيَكَتَبَتْ
هُمْكَتَبُواهُنَّكَتَبْنَ

What are the moods of the Arabic present tense?

The present tense (المضارع) changes its ending by mood:

  • Indicative, the normal statement: يَكْتُبُ he writes.
  • Subjunctive (المنصوب), after أَنْ and لَنْ: لَنْ يَكْتُبَ he will not write.
  • Jussive (المجزوم), after لَمْ and the prohibitive لَا: لَمْ يَكْتُبْ he did not write.

How do you form the Arabic imperative?

The imperative (الأمر) is built from the jussive: اُكْتُبْ to one man, اُكْتُبِي to one woman, اُكْتُبَا to two, اُكْتُبُوا to men, اُكْتُبْنَ to women.

What are the emphatic and passive forms?

The emphatic (energetic) form adds نَّ for strong assertion: لَيَكْتُبَنَّ he will surely write. The passive changes the vowels: كُتِبَ it was written, يُكْتَبُ it is written.
