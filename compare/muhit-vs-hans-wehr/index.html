Muhit vs Hans Wehr: which Arabic dictionary should you use?

By The Muhit team · 7 October 2026

What is Hans Wehr?

Hans Wehr's A Dictionary of Modern Written Arabic, edited in English by J. Milton Cowan, is the standard printed Arabic-English dictionary for students. It is thorough, carefully edited and arranged by root.

Looking up a word: by root or as you see it

In Hans Wehr you find a word under its root, so مَكْتَبَة is under ك ت ب. That keeps families together but means you must work out the root first. In Muhit you type the word as you see it, with or without vowels, or type English, and the root is shown on the word's page. See how to find an Arabic root.

Verb conjugation

Hans Wehr gives a verb's form number (I to X) and its present-tense vowel, and expects you to know the patterns. Muhit shows a full table for each verb: every person, tense and mood, active and passive. See the conjugation guide.

Depth of definitions

Hans Wehr is generally more detailed, especially for rare words, idioms and fine shades of meaning. Muhit focuses on clear, numbered meanings and short vowelled example sentences.

Practice and study

A printed dictionary is a reference. Muhit adds folders, flashcards and quizzes with spaced repetition, plus word lists for the Madinah and Bayna Yadayk courses.

Which should you use?

Both, for different jobs. Use Muhit for fast lookups on your phone, conjugation and daily review; keep Hans Wehr for deep reading and research. Muhit works offline and needs no account.
